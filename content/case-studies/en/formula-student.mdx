## Context
UPM Racing competes in **Formula Student**, where teams **design, build, and validate** a race car under strict regulations. The yearly cycle is end‑to‑end: requirements → design → manufacturing → testing → competition.

At competition:
- Technical inspection and documentation (regulatory compliance).
- Dynamic tests (brake test, slalom, endurance).
- Static defenses before judges (design, cost, organization).

Motivation: complement theory with real engineering, teamwork, and delivery pressure.

## Deep dives

### Deep dive — Mechanical Engineer: brake redesign
[Go to standalone case study: Brake System Redesign →](/projects/formula-brake-redesign)

**Situation:** during technical inspection, a hard press caused a **critical failure** of the brake system.  
**Objective:** redesign it to be **robust**, adjustable, and manufacturable.

**What I did**
- CAD design and **interference checks**.
- Adapted geometry for **3‑axis manufacturing**.
- Drawings + tolerances (hands‑on with machinist feedback).
- Selected bushings/friction components.
- Coordinated chassis mounting constraints.

**Result**
- Reinforced system that **passed the “hard press”** and later validation.
- Preserved balance bar and braking performance.
- Key lesson: **design for manufacturing** and real tolerances.

### Deep dive — Configuration Manager: CAD governance in 3DEXPERIENCE
**Problem:** car CAD was “owned by two experts,” fragile dependencies, limited coordination across divisions.

**What I implemented**
- **Impact analysis** required before CAD changes.
- **Skeleton guide:** what to reference and how to avoid toxic dependencies.
- **Division permissions** (collaborative spaces) to work in context safely.
- **Controlled reviews** + “branch/merge” at subassembly level.
- Lightweight **Definition of Ready/Done** (impact + review closed).
- Stable **naming convention** (UPMR) to avoid mass renames.

**Result**
- More parallel work without breaking the model.
- Better change traceability and rollback.

**Hard lesson: front wing vs chassis interference**
During assembly, the front wing **interfered** with the nose/chassis due to outdated versions across divisions.
- **Impact:** rework and weaker design defense.
- **Change:** cross‑division visibility required before interface changes.

## Learnings
- **Ownership without title** in low‑process environments.
- **End‑to‑end engineering:** requirements → design → manufacturing → testing → competition.
- **CM/Change mindset** applied to CAD and releases.
- **Design for manufacture** and real tolerances.

**What I’d do differently**
- Run mock scrutineering earlier.
- Keep regulations as living checklists per subsystem.
- Add decision gates with dates to avoid late blocks.
- Do cross‑division impact analysis before interface changes.
